PUBG Mobile Account Trading: Rules & Safety

PUBG Mobile account listings often emphasize rare outfits, upgradeable weapons and rank history. Before looking at those features, understand the more fundamental question: are accounts transferable under the publisher's rules?

Publisher rule: PUBG Mobile states that an account is personal and may not be gifted, lent or transferred to another person. Its account-security guidance describes unauthorized account trading as risky. A private sale does not override those rules.

What the publisher says about transfers

According to the PUBG Mobile End User License Agreement, access to an account is personal. It prohibits gifting, lending or transferring it to another person. A listing that says "full access", "secure" or "guaranteed" cannot establish the publisher's permission for a transfer. Account access or game items may be lost if rules are enforced.

PUBG Mobile's account-risk advisory also explains why account trading can expose linked account credentials and allow other parties to change access. These are separate from the risk that the game publisher may restrict the account itself.

Why screenshots cannot prove control of an account

Images of an inventory or lobby show what was visible when the image was taken. They do not prove who created the account, whether a third-party login is still linked, whether a prior owner can regain access, or whether the item remains present today. Video clips have the same limitation. Screenshots can be edited, reused or become outdated as inventories change.

Even real-time access does not settle ownership. An email address, social login or recovery mechanism outside the game may remain controlled by someone else. An account could be locked, recovered or banned after a transaction. There is no reliable screenshot-only method for eliminating those risks.

Warning signs in account listings

  • Urgent pressure: the seller insists on instant payment before you can read the full terms or ask questions.
  • Unverifiable guarantees: claims that a transfer is "100% ban-proof", "never recoverable" or officially approved without publisher evidence.
  • Requests for secrets: demands for passwords, one-time codes, recovery codes or control of unrelated personal accounts.
  • Price or identity changes: the advertised item changes after payment or the seller switches to a different account without explanation.
  • Inconsistent screenshots: level, inventory totals, region or login details do not match across the listing materials.

Protect an account you already own

Use a unique password for linked login services, enable multi-factor authentication wherever supported, secure recovery email addresses and review account access sessions. Never give a verification code to a seller, buyer, supposed moderator or customer-support impersonator. Keep the official game's client and operating system updated; avoid downloaded "skin unlockers" and other modified game files that may expose credentials or result in penalties.

If you receive an unexpected login alert, secure your linked provider accounts first, change passwords through their official websites and revoke unrecognized sessions where that option exists. Record the time and messages involved. Do not send identity documents or passwords to an unsolicited chat contact.

If an account is compromised

  1. Use your linked email or login provider's official recovery process and check its security alerts.
  2. Contact PUBG Mobile support through official channels. Provide only the documentation that support requests.
  3. Preserve payment confirmations and relevant chat records for a dispute, but remove passwords and personal details before sharing screenshots with anyone.
  4. Report impersonation or a fraudulent transaction to the relevant payment provider. Avoid paying a second stranger who promises to recover your account.

Understanding a listing without endorsing a transfer

Terms such as "Level 70", "M416 Glacier Lv. 7", "three X-Suits", or "Conqueror" refer to different attributes: progression, a particular upgrade level, cosmetic ownership and competitive history. They do not establish legal ownership or transferability. See the skin terminology guide for a closer breakdown.

An account listing can be useful as an example of game cosmetics, but deciding whether to participate in a transaction requires considering the publisher's rules and risk of losing access. When in doubt, keep using an account you created and control.
