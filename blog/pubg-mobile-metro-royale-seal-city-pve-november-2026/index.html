PUBG Mobile Metro Royale Seal City: New PvE Map Explained

Published November 8, 2026 · By Digital Racer

PUBG Mobile is preparing a different kind of Metro Royale challenge. Seal City, announced for November 13, 2026, is a PvE map centered on an infected coastal town. The Version 4.7 beta outlined new enemies, scavenging rules and environmental changes. This preview explains what is confirmed, what was only tested, and how to approach the mode without assuming the final rewards or difficulty.

Seal City release date and setting

The PUBG Mobile team announced that Seal City is coming on November 13, one day after the scheduled Version 4.7 update. The announcement describes a ruined city overrun by infected enemies. This is a Metro Royale PvE experience, not simply a replacement for ordinary Classic Mode Erangel. Check Metro Royale's map selector after the launch to confirm access and any unlock conditions.

The third beta announcement called Seal City a 1.8 by 1.8 map with a coastal town, autumn scenery and a zombie outbreak. Its scale and environment were part of a test, so small changes are possible before general release. The key distinction is that the environment and enemies are designed to reward planning and survival rather than only conventional player-versus-player gunfights.

Day and night change how encounters may unfold

Version 4.7 beta information describes a day-night cycle that changes the look of the environment and the behavior of PvE enemies. It also mentions enemy dormancy. This could make route timing important: a path that appears quiet at one point may become dangerous later. Players should scout rather than assuming every route stays safe for the entire session.

A cautious first run is better than rushing toward the largest visible group of enemies. Learn where you can retreat, where cover is available and how quickly your team can regroup. If the final map retains nighttime-specific threats, keep track of environmental cues and make room for unexpected enemies rather than carrying only one strategy.

What bosses and infected enemies were shown in beta?

The third beta notice listed several bosses and special creatures, including a Reaper Scorpion, an Aberrant Vine and other unusual infected threats. It also included heavily armed elite enemies and ordinary zombie variations. This is a preview roster, not a guaranteed final spawn chart. Specific enemy names, health values or exact locations may differ at launch.

In a survival-oriented map, it is useful to identify whether a creature threatens at close range, forces players from cover or distracts the team while others approach. Do not use an unverified online damage chart as a substitute for observing attacks. Coordinating fire and retreat paths may matter more than trying to defeat every enemy immediately.

New items could change how groups avoid enemies

The tested Scream Grenade was described as drawing nearby PvE enemies and briefly diverting their attention. A separate Stealth Serum was described as making players temporarily ignored by PvE enemies. If these items appear in the final version, they could support different approaches to difficult encounters: drawing threats away from a corridor or moving carefully through a crowded area.

Neither item should be treated as permanent invisibility or immunity. Their duration, supply and interaction with specific enemies were not fully established for the finished release in the preview material. Try them in lower-risk encounters before depending on them during an important loot run. Keep a fallback plan if an ability ends unexpectedly.

Scavenging and loadout changes in the test

Beta documentation described Equipment Vouchers for quicker loadout setup, more convenient pre-match preparation and additional search containers. It also previewed a redesigned backpack layout and a more staged search process in which a container reveals items progressively instead of immediately. Those adjustments could make exposure time during looting a deliberate risk.

High-pressure zones and advanced supply zones also appeared in the test design, with stronger enemies or better potential supplies. Higher-value areas naturally invite more danger. A practical team should decide in advance whether an extraction-focused run is worth risking for a specific loot target, rather than being distracted by every high-value location.

How to prepare for the new PvE map

Before November 13, review Metro Royale controls, your usual gear setup and how teammates communicate target priorities. On the first day, verify which map and enemy mechanics are actually live. Start with a conservative route, note the location of repeatable hazards and learn the extraction or completion rules shown by the in-game interface. Avoid trusting final loot values from beta screenshots.

Seal City should also be an interesting test of device performance, since crowds of infected and changing environments can create different workloads from standard matches. Mobile players should use reliable graphics settings. Emulator players should keep the official game and emulator current, and avoid modified clients that promise hidden map access before release.

  • Announced launch: November 13, 2026.
  • Seal City is a Metro Royale PvE map with an infected-coastal-town theme.
  • Day-night behavior, bosses and special items were detailed in beta tests.
  • Final loot tables, item duration and access rules must be verified in-game.
  • Plan scouting and safe extraction before attempting aggressive loot runs.
