GTA 6 Radio Stations and Podcasts: Leonida Music Guide

GTA VI is bringing music into more of the player's day than just a car journey. Rockstar's October 8 announcement showcased six radio stations and described on-demand podcasts and phone-connected headphones for Jason and Lucia. Here is what is officially announced, which genres appear in the preview and what remains unknown until the November 19 game release.

Six featured stations give Leonida distinct sounds

Rockstar's published selection includes Cocoteo FM, Back Country, Afro Bank FM, The Chamber, Flash FM and Dirty South Classics. They span Latin music, country, African sounds, metal, pop and regional rap. These are descriptions of six showcased stations, not a complete and final inventory of every frequency in the game.

A station's identity is more than its playlist. Hosts, presentation, interruptions and local flavor can help make a fictional city feel lived in. Before launch, listeners should avoid treating unofficial track lists as confirmed songs merely because a musician's style fits a station's genre.

Cocoteo FM and Afro Bank FM

Cocoteo FM is presented as modern Latin programming associated with El Martillo and La Gata. It fits the musical variety Rockstar is showcasing in Vice City and beyond. Fans interested in that station can learn its announced genre without assuming any particular unreleased song is included.

Afro Bank FM is described around amapiano, African classics and related sounds from Burna and Palmsy. The variety matters because GTA VI's radio is being advertised as broader than a narrow period-piece soundtrack. It offers world-building through contemporary and regional musical identities.

Back Country and The Chamber

Back Country is promoted as country music suitable for an airboat journey, with MW and Delta Dawne. Its presentation hints at a musical identity for regions beyond the city's nightlife. That does not establish specific road locations or the behavior of every vehicle in the final release.

The Chamber is a metal-oriented station with DJ Kerry and DJ Tom. For players who associate GTA radio with genre-specific hosts, this suggests that musical personalities remain part of the series. Do not infer full playlists from the names alone; Rockstar can announce more selections closer to launch.

Flash FM and Dirty South Classics

Flash FM returns as a recognizable Vice City institution in Rockstar's marketing, featuring pop music and hosts Robyn and Alex. Its presence connects the new game to the series' radio heritage while still leaving room for a contemporary soundtrack. Familiar branding does not imply the complete playlist from an older game will return.

Dirty South Classics emphasizes regional rap and hip-hop, with Trick and Trina. It complements the other stations and gives a distinct musical frame for travel through Leonida. Listen for how the final game uses radio dialogue and presentation to connect places, characters and stories.

Podcasts and mobile listening are new highlights

Rockstar says GTA VI introduces on-demand podcasts alongside radio. These can be listened to in vehicles and on foot, using headphones or earbuds connected to characters' smartphones. That differs from relying entirely on a vehicle's stereo and could change how players encounter spoken-world content while exploring on foot.

The announcement does not provide a complete podcast catalog or promise that every broadcast can be replayed at any time. Players should inspect the final phone and audio interface after release. The distinction between live stations and selectable on-demand episodes will be worth testing in normal gameplay.

What radio news does not reveal

Music marketing cannot confirm the full map, every character mission or online service features. A song associated with a trailer does not necessarily play on a named station during a particular mission. Likewise, promotional station artwork should not be used to infer exact playable building interiors.

Rockstar has scheduled GTA VI for November 19 on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. This article focuses on the announced music experience and avoids publishing lyrics, copied playlists or claims of leaked content. After launch, a verified station guide can document the controls that actually work.
