GTA 6 Soundtrack: Justice Score and December Album Release

Published November 8, 2026 · By Digital Racer

Music is part of GTA VI's official launch campaign, but there are several different releases to keep straight. Rockstar announced that French electronic duo Justice created the game's original score and scheduled a 30-track album for December 11. The game itself is scheduled for November 19, so the album release is a separate date. Here is the difference between score, radio and companion music.

Justice composed the game's original score

Rockstar's October 29 announcement names Grammy-winning duo Justice, Gaspard Augé and Xavier de Rosnay, as composers of the GTA VI original score. The publisher describes adaptive compositions that change with the way players engage with the world and with Jason and Lucia's unfolding story.

An adaptive score is not identical to a fixed radio playlist. It can respond to the action or narrative context rather than playing a single song from beginning to end. Rockstar has not published technical details for every transition, so descriptions of a specific chase or mission cue before launch would be speculation.

Why the December 11 date matters

The official original-score album is scheduled for December 11, 2026 on major streaming services and physical formats, several weeks after the November 19 game launch. Rockstar describes 30 tracks plus digital bonus material across album formats. That makes the album a distinct release from the game itself.

An album date is not a game update or a new character unlock. Players should not interpret the release as a promise that all score material is inaccessible in the game beforehand. The album is an opportunity to listen to the official compositions separately from the interactive experience.

Flower in a Hurricane and the launch trailer

Rockstar's October launch trailer featured Flower in a Hurricane, a Justice composition with Tame Impala, SZA and King Gizzard & The Lizard Wizard credited on the published single. The accompanying campaign presented the track as part of the game's music, adding an identifiable piece of original material to the launch promotion.

The presence of a song in a trailer does not tell us exactly where it will appear within the playable story. Trailer editing and in-game music placement serve different purposes. Avoid assigning it to an invented mission or final-scene moment before Rockstar or the released game confirms the context.

Pusher and additional soundtrack material

The October 29 announcement also introduced a single titled Pusher featuring Gesaffelstein. Its release gives listeners another example of the project's sound ahead of the full album. That is supported by the publisher's announcement; an exhaustive online track list should still be checked against the final album packaging.

Rockstar has also promoted broader GTA VI album material and music-related merchandise, but those releases are separate from the adaptive original score and from in-game radio stations. Keeping the categories separate helps readers understand what they are buying or pre-saving.

How score, soundtrack and radio differ

The original score is music composed to accompany the game's scenes and interactions. In-game radio provides curated station programming, while a separate soundtrack album can collect selected songs associated with the world. Some material may appear in multiple contexts, but the labels are not interchangeable.

For example, an artist credited in a trailer does not automatically become the host of an in-game station. An official track on a record does not establish that every GTA VI platform will include a particular downloadable music file. Look at the product listing and game interface for the exact offering.

What is confirmed ahead of November 19

The names of the score composers, album date and selected singles are official announcements. The detailed timing of music inside every gameplay situation remains something players will discover after launch. This guide deliberately avoids rumors about supposedly leaked scenes tied to particular songs.

DigitalRacer's coverage will separate the November 19 console launch from the December 11 score-album launch. Neither date establishes a PC release schedule. Players interested in the music can enjoy the announced work without treating promotional copy as proof of unannounced gameplay systems.
