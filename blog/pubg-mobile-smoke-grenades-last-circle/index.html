PUBG Mobile Smoke Grenades in the Final Circle: Placement and Timing

Published November 18, 2026 · By Digital Racer

Use smoke to cross sightlines, revive and reposition while respecting its limits. This Digital Racer guide focuses on decisions a player can check in ordinary gameplay, not secret unlocks or promises of certain results. The exact conditions depend on mode, device and current update, so begin with the rules and settings that the official game actually exposes. Aim for a repeatable process that improves awareness, safety and consistency instead of copying a single dramatic clip.

Identify the threatening angle

Before throwing a smoke, determine where enemies can actually see the squad. Placing it randomly may obscure your own view while leaving the enemy's strongest angle open. Mark the intended destination and cover route.

Choose a purpose for each smoke

A revive, vehicle dismount and open-field crossing need different cloud locations. Do not spend all grenades at the first sign of gunfire. Keep at least one option for a later change in the circle when possible.

Understand the cover limitation

Smoke blocks visibility but not bullets, explosives or vehicle collisions. Skilled opponents can spray predictable positions or wait at the cloud edge. Avoid standing still in the center when a safer hard-cover location exists.

Move as the cloud develops

Timing matters because a smoke takes time to obscure an area and eventually dissipates. Coordinate the crossing so teammates are ready, not moving in unrelated directions. Reassess if wind-like visual effects or obstacles alter visibility.

Avoid unnecessary information leaks

Throwing smoke can announce your approximate location and intention. A cloud near the only doorway may invite grenades or a flank. Consider whether a quiet rotation using terrain would be less conspicuous.

Review utility efficiency

After a match, note which throw actually interrupted an enemy sightline and which merely felt reassuring. Practice a small number of predictable placements before trying complex tricks. Smoke discipline should support positioning, not replace it.

How to test the advice without overreacting

For a useful trial, choose a comparable match scenario and identify the first decision you can control. Keep teammates informed and favor hard cover over a dramatic shortcut. If opponents behave differently, adapt to their actual angles and timing rather than forcing a scripted tactic. Review one avoidable mistake afterwards. The objective is a decision process that works across many lobbies, not a highlight clip or a supposed guaranteed result.
